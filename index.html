Sanskrit Phonetic & Acoustic Experiment Lab

Systematic Organization Analysis: Sanskrit sounds are systematically organized according to their articulation patterns and phonetic properties. This organization supports pronunciation training, language learning, and provides a structured phonetic foundation for speech-processing applications.

Note on ऋ/ॠ: the vocalic R vowels rarely occur as standalone syllables in real Sanskrit/Hindi text, so isolated TTS often mispronounces them (sometimes reading ऋ as the consonant र instead). Their buttons instead speak real words (ऋषि, पितॄन्) so you hear the authentic vocalic-r sound in context.

Together with the vowels and the five stop-vargas above, these two groups complete the traditional articulation-based classification of Sanskrit sounds (Kaṇṭhya → Oṣṭhya, plus Antaḥsthā and Ūṣman).

Articulation Pattern Analysis

Sanskrit consonants are systematically organized according to their place of articulation and phonetic properties. This classification helps learners understand where and how each sound is produced.

  • Guttural (Kanthya): Sounds produced mainly toward the throat/back of the vocal tract.
  • Palatal (Tālavya): Sounds produced with the tongue approaching the palate.
  • Retroflex (Mūrdhanya): Sounds produced with the tongue curled toward the roof of the mouth.
  • Dental (Dantya): Sounds produced with the tongue near the teeth.
  • Labial (Oṣṭhya): Sounds produced mainly using the lips.
  • Semivowels (Antaḥsthā): य र ल व — produced with a partial constriction, intermediate between vowels and consonants.
  • Sibilants & Aspirate (Ūṣman): श ष स ह — produced with continuous airflow/friction rather than a full stop.

Applications

The systematic organization of Sanskrit sounds supports pronunciation training by allowing learners to practice individual sounds and compare their articulation patterns. It supports language learning by organizing vowels and consonants into easily understandable phonetic groups. In speech processing, phonetic classification and acoustic waveform analysis can provide useful information for applications such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, pronunciation assessment, and language-learning systems.
