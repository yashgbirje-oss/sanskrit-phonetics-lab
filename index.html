Articulation Pattern Analysis

Sanskrit consonants are systematically organized according to their place of articulation and phonetic properties. This classification helps learners understand where and how each sound is produced.

  • Guttural: Produced mainly toward the throat/back of the vocal tract.
  • Palatal: Produced with the tongue approaching the palate.
  • Retroflex: Produced with the tongue curled toward the roof of the mouth.
  • Dental: Produced with the tongue near the teeth.
  • Labial: Produced mainly using the lips.
  • Semivowels: य र ल व — produced with partial constriction.
  • Ūṣman: श ष स ह — produced with continuous airflow and friction.

Applications

The systematic organization of Sanskrit sounds supports pronunciation training by allowing learners to practice individual sounds and compare their articulation patterns. It supports language learning by organizing vowels and consonants into understandable phonetic groups. In speech processing, phonetic classification and acoustic waveform analysis can provide useful information for applications such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, pronunciation assessment, and language-learning systems.
